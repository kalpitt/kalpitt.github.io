One tap installs banking malware that reads OTPs and empties accounts. It targets the family members least able to spot it.

What Saavdhan does

The promises that never change

  • Fully offline.पूरी तरह offline। The app has no internet permission, so Android itself stops it from going online. The build fails if that permission ever sneaks in.
  • It guides you. It never pretends.रास्ता दिखाता है, दिखावा नहीं करता। Android only lets you, the owner, switch off another app's powers. Saavdhan opens the right screen and coaches the final tap, with fallbacks for Samsung, Xiaomi, Oppo, Vivo and Huawei phones. It never fakes an automatic fix.
  • Every warning explains itself.हर चेतावनी अपना कारण बताती है। Each result lists its reasons in plain words, in Hindi or English, not just a score.

What v0.7.0 does

  • One-tap scan of every installed app, with a risk level and reasons. You choose Hindi or English inside the app, whatever the phone's language.
  • A guided clean-up checklist that removes a dangerous app's powers before uninstalling it, then confirms the app is really gone.
  • A watchdog that warns you when a newly installed app looks dangerous, the moment that matters during a live scam.
  • Messenger-install detection that flags apps installed from WhatsApp and similar apps, the signature of this scam.
  • “Send result to family”: a summary you can share without the internet, so a son or daughter in another city can see what the scan found.

A signed release is on GitHub. The Play Store listing is prepared but not yet filed.

What it doesn't do

  • No malware database. It judges apps by how they behave and what they ask for, so it can raise false alarms and can miss brand-new threats.
  • No online updates. Detection rules ship inside the app and only change with an app update. That's the price of never going online.
  • Some switches only you can flip. Android keeps them for the phone's owner, and Saavdhan says so.
  • Money already stolen? First call 1930, India's cyber-crime helpline, and your bank.

For engineers

The risk engine is plain Kotlin with no Android code, so it gives the same answer every time and is easy to test. It scores clear signals: dangerous combinations of permissions, apps installed from a messenger instead of the Play Store, names that imitate trusted brands, and missing trusted signatures. Every verdict carries its ranked reasons, which is what lets the app explain itself. The build fails if the internet permission appears, or if any on-screen line is missing its Hindi version.

Why I built it

Scam APKs reached my own family's WhatsApp. Security apps assume a technical user, and most want internet access and personal data, which is exactly why families hesitate to install them. Saavdhan is what I wanted my family to have: promises checked by the build, warnings in their language, and honesty about what it can't do.
